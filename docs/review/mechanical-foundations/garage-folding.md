# Garage tilt-up and bifold mechanical audit

This work examines generated geometry and native MuJoCo mechanics. It includes no appearance renders, human-motion approval, structural certification, or measured manufacturer force curves. Private generated evidence is under `out/mechanical-foundations/garage-folding/`; source changes never hand-edit `assets/`.

## Tilt-up: replace the stationary waist-height hinge

The previous tilt-up template put the sole panel hinge at half the leaf height. At 88° the open db0005-style panel remained near 1.06 m, directly across the passage. Moving that same fixed hinge upward would not supply the required lifting mechanism.

The replacement is an original retractable up-and-over linkage: a travelling top roller carriage, a hinged rigid panel, two side lifting arms with actual bored pivot eyes and pins, two native extension counterbalance springs, horizontal C tracks, side beams and rear support posts. Two native point constraints connect the arm tips to the panel brackets. Rollers have independent spin bearings and 5 mm clearance to the upper channel flange. The lower flange carries their tread. Closed-loop dynamics use MuJoCo constraints and contacts; the analytic resolver is only for prescribed-pose inspection and replay.

The topology is informed by [Garador's retractable fitting instructions](https://www.garador.co.uk/media/eawdrr4j/retractable.pdf), available through its [official fitting-instructions collection](https://www.garador.co.uk/installer-support/fitting-instructions/). Dimensions, support geometry and spring calibration are original DoorBench engineering choices. This is a retractable installation, not a canopy mechanism or a reproduction of manufacturer CAD. Springs are sized by energy to the authored counterbalance fraction and remain in tension through the whole travel; that fraction does not imply an exact counterbalance torque at every angle.

The native test caught a defect that static clearance did not: a 40 mm wheel between precisely 40 mm track faces developed opposing friction contacts and stalled db0284. A real spin bearing and upper running clearance corrected the jam. No contact exclusion, reduced collision geometry, forced qpos update, or QA threshold waiver was used to make that test pass.

## Bifold: guided independent banks and usable pulls

All 30 bifolds now have an actual open top channel, lower pivot plates/pins, top pivots/sockets, lead guide stems and nylon guide rollers. The original panel coupling remains `fold = −2 × pivot`; four-panel doors contain two independent two-panel banks. No equality couples the left and right banks.

[P C Henderson's Bifold product documentation](https://www.pchenderson.com/bifold/) describes both two-panel and two-plus-two arrangements. Its [official hardware drawing](https://www.pchenderson.com/media/documents/BIFOLD-FOLDING-WARDROBE-DOOR-GEAR-LVL-A.pdf) documents upper guides and lower/upper pivots. This is topology evidence; DoorBench does not claim every generated material/weight uses that exact manufacturer's rated kit.

The [Johnson 100FD installation drawing, page 2](https://www.johnsonhardware.com/content/Images/uploaded/Documentation/100FDCatalogPage.pdf) was downloaded and personally inspected. It places the knob centrally on the leading panel. DoorBench pulls now follow that layout, rather than sitting beside the track-constrained free edge with almost no useful outward leverage. Louvered leading panels have a solid crossrail supporting the pull.

For every bank of all 30 variants, the signed constrained grip Jacobian gives positive opening leverage under an outward pull: the minimum is 35.54 mm per radian. At 45 positions per bank the lead guide stays on its fixed world track plane within 1 μm; the other bank stays exactly closed when only one bank is prescribed. Swept structural and running-clearance checks cover both banks. The reference policy separately opens each bank using its real grip and holds previously opened banks; that sequencing is owned by the parent task.

The first circulating hand-chain link uses a real native free joint: seven world-pose coordinates (XYZ plus WXYZ quaternion), six velocity coordinates and the link’s actual geometric inertia.

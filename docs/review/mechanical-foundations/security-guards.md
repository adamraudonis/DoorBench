# Contact-operated chains and swing guards

The six security devices have installed frame and leaf hardware in every native tier, including the initially disengaged chain on DB0117. Leaf hinges retain their full travel. A headed chain end against a real slotted keeper, or a pivoting slotted bar against a curved keeper, supplies the holding contact. No shortened door range or environment-toggled weld supplies security.

The layout is informed by the manufacturer’s [Ives 481 installation instructions](https://us.allegion.com/content/dam/allegion-us-2/web-files/ives/installation-documents/Ives_481_Chain_Door_Guard_Installation_Instructions_107824.pdf) and [Ives chain and bar guard descriptions](https://www.iveshardware.com/en/products/latches-catches-and-bolts/chain-and-bar-door-guards.html). The geometry is original and simplified. No OEM CAD, strength rating, fastener pullout model or resistance-to-bypass certification is incorporated.

The chain has eight articulated steel-wire loops, an actual bored frame anchor and a three-axis terminal swivel. The authored pivot span is 148 mm. A 14 mm release opening admits the 8 mm head; the 5 mm slot retains its 3.6 mm neck. The finger grip is hollow around an inner swivel ball and bored intermediate ring. Direct distance tests across 27 combinations of the three swivel coordinates check the nested material clearance despite native parent-body filtering. Native joints approximate welded-link and internal swivel articulation; they do not simulate the material contact inside every individual bearing. Remote links and the keeper remain collidable. The one explicit last-link/head exclusion represents adjacent members of the compound swivel, not a blanket chain or keeper exemption. Body inertia is authored from geometric mass without the usual joint armature floor, and the moving mechanism mass is included in the assembly budget. The shared MJCF exporter still applies its existing 1e−9 kg·m² minimum principal inertia to the very small terminal ring and ball; their native rotational inertia is therefore numerically bounded, while the URDF retains the smaller authored values.

The bar guard has a 120.65 mm arm, separate upper/lower frame pivots, an open slotted arm and a curved leaf-mounted keeper. Its actual hinge moves through 180 degrees to park beside the jamb. The leaf-side keeper remains installed while parked.

Both types are mounted on the interior opening face. The post-generation correction changes the swing direction of DB0159, DB0481 and DB0781 to match their preserved interior/exterior approach choices; it does not move the guard to the robot-facing side. DB0481 and DB0727 are accessible from the authored robot approach. DB0117, DB0159, DB0781 and DB0953 are not. The latter doors cannot claim an outside robot can manually release their interior guard merely because an inside-service fixture can do so.

The service sequence closes the leaf, slides a chain head to its enlarged opening, then withdraws it normally; the bar instead rotates to its parked angle. The chain controller targets the actual grip surface at the position implied by the desired head pose, and damps that same surface's velocity. The shortest world orientation error uses 0.2 Nm/rad stiffness and 0.0002 Nms/rad damping, with an unchanged 0.02 Nm resultant grasp-torque cap. The physical force's offset also creates its real moment; no numerical moment cancellation is applied. Earlier head-centre feedback applied at the offset grip could spin the loose fitting during regrasp despite a correct commanded head path.

Re-engagement first moves the measured dangling fitting outward/upward, then carries it through a clear plane with a 2 mm reserve inside the chain's finite reach. Approach toward the keeper requires measured head position within 3 mm, orientation within 0.15 rad, and projected keyhole error within 2.9 mm. Failed alignment is held for a bounded interval and then fails the proof; it never advances merely because a timer elapsed. At the enlarged opening, the measured finite neck must span both retaining-lip faces with at least 0.1 mm material clearance, including its tilted elliptical section. The head also must meet the original 0.6 mm vertical slot condition. Sliding to the retained end must finish within 3 mm. Phase receipts include actual head/orientation/contact measurements and the two lip-face clearance values. These are measured native trajectories, not assertions that every commanded straight segment is exactly tracked.

The dedicated proof uses a fresh native state, retained contacts and bounded forces through `mj_step`, with no intermediate pose assignment. It performs two release/open/close/re-engage/load cycles. Ordinary latches are held withdrawn so this fixture isolates the security device. Actual authored closer torque and valve behavior remain active. DB0159’s automatic actuator is explicitly disabled only in the private manual-service fixture. Leaf fixture torque is capped at 100 Nm, grasp force at 20 N per axis and grasp torque at 0.02 Nm. Every contact is measured; penetration above 1 mm or any native warning fails the proof. Removing the bar end or chain slot lips is a causal negative: the device then fails its retention load test. Closing the real enlarged keyhole to the slot width must also fail the complete native cycle. The native time-step ceilings are 25 microseconds for chains and 100 microseconds for bars; this is not a real-time-performance claim.

All authored steel chain, head and keeper geometries now declare native contact priority 1, retaining `solref=(0.0002, 1)` and `solimp=(0.95, 0.95, 0.0001)` against generic jamb stock. MuJoCo otherwise averages equal-priority material settings; its [contact parameter rules](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/modeling.html#contact-parameters) explain this behavior. This is an explicit stiff steel-contact solver scope, with every name bound in `contact_solver_scope`. Preflight rejects missing declarations, lowered compiled priority or softened compiled contact settings. The 128 explicit adjacent wire/eye pairs retain the same stiff parameters. No head, wire or jamb contact is removed, and the penetration gate stays at 1 mm. Earlier head-only priority diagnostics are retained under `out/mechanical-foundations/security/priority-comparison.json` and `adjacent-repair/`; they do not approve the later corrected geometry.

The four chain models also declare `native_integrator="implicit"`. Full implicit integration includes the velocity derivatives of coupled rotational forces omitted by `implicitfast`; MuJoCo specifically identifies [multi-link pendula](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html#numerical-integration) as a case benefiting from this choice. In the earlier installation, at 25 microseconds, DB0781's `implicitfast` regrasp developed runaway joint speeds and a native acceleration warning. The same exported geometry, forces and contacts passed both cycles with full implicit integration, with 0.945 mm maximum penetration. The timestep reduction alone was therefore insufficient. The dedicated gate requires both the 25 microsecond ceiling and full implicit mode for chains, and it retains the failures from the superseded settings.

The reference cycle proof is specific to the authored MJCF contact parameters, inertia serialization and integration settings. Successful URDF loading verifies articulated structure and positive inertias, not equivalent contact, spring or guard-cycle behavior in another engine. USD/URDF users need to supply and validate their engine's corresponding solver and mechanism behavior; no cross-engine security or release equivalence is claimed.

The public entry point is `doorbench.security_mechanics_qa.run_security_service_qa(model, metadata, opening_preload=0., opening_stiffness=0.)`. It copies both native model and metadata, compiles the copied pinion/track passive fields, disables automatic gains/biases only on that copy and restores the caller's passive callback even on failure. Its receipt records before/after source-model binary hashes and the exact disabled actuator parameters. It runs synchronously: concurrent MuJoCo calls from other threads are unsupported because MuJoCo's passive callback is process-global. The unchanged lower-level `run_security_mechanics_qa` remains available for explicit fixtures and negative tests; callers of that lower-level API must install the appropriate passive fields themselves.

Other reproduction tools are the focused `tests/test_security_guards.py` and local full-source driver `out/mechanical-foundations/security/final_native.py`. That driver exports privately, verifies full/simple/minimal MJCF and URDF loading, binds spec/model/XML hashes and records its disabled-actuator fixture explicitly. These are mechanism checks, not a robot benchmark, human-motion validation or whole-door certificate.

Whole-geometry inspection corrected three hidden defects before the present proof. The frame bracket now has a genuinely subtracted 7 mm bore around its 6 mm pin; named-stock distance tests preserve 0.5 mm clearance across five swivel angles. Neighboring chain loops now have a 13 mm outer width and a perpendicular anchor eye, with all 128 adjacent wire/eye contacts explicitly enabled despite MuJoCo's usual parent/child filter. DB0781's anchor plate sits wholly on its real casing face, and that supporting casing remains collidable in all three tiers. Restoring the old solid bracket, narrow loops or buried anchor plate fails focused geometric tests. No additional contact exclusion was introduced.

### Integrated task follow-up

The four refreshed chain-door sources passed whole-door generation QA. Initial primary-task recordings passed DB0117, DB0781 and DB0953; DB0481 failed when the input controller updated at 4 ms while its mechanism stepped at 25 microseconds. Keeping the same source model, geometry, masses, springs and input caps, refreshing the actual-site forces each native step completed DB0481's unlock-and-traverse task in 13.978 native seconds with no damage or MuJoCo warnings. The force law was not strengthened.

The replacement recording is retained under `security/runtime-native-cadence/reference-motions/`; its index SHA256 is `14234e46438b065c85b894aa8974f0afd0194a393d9df98ea3f6f9e4bb2db2cf`. Every recorded state and actual-site command passed source/frame correspondence validation. This validator explicitly requests only DB0481 from the four-door manifest; it does not claim that this one-clip index covers all four. The earlier failed run remains available. These are abstract mechanism-oracle tasks, not embodied human demonstrations.

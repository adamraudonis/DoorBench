# Supported automatic-door activation pads

Eleven models have two physical activation pads each: DB0011, DB0136, DB0153, DB0175, DB0225, DB0433, DB0474, DB0629, DB0801, DB0829 and DB0939. Each now has an 8 mm steel stem through a prepared 9.5 mm guide in a wall-mounted plate. The round cap is 104 mm across and the plate is 130 mm square. These are original generic assemblies, not reproductions or certified equivalents of a particular manufacturer's product. The earlier moving pad had no stem between it and the plate.

The 4 mm native stroke retains a 0.5 mm minimum cap-to-plate gap and 0.75 mm stem-to-guide clearance. The surface press site moves with the cap. Its joint remains an operator, with the original `activation_button_n_slide` / `activation_button_p_slide` names and `activation_button_n_push` / `activation_button_p_push` sites. The electrical threshold remains 2 mm; only the near-face site is accessible from the authored approach. No electrical contact block, fastening strength or switch durability is simulated. The native step ceiling is 0.5 ms, preserving any stricter existing model requirement.

DB0474's 4.84 m aperture left only 35 mm of side wall within the previous 5 m environment envelope, so its old station was beyond the wall. The station-side wall now extends outward far enough to support the complete plate with 20 mm spare stock. Its aperture-side edge, height and thickness are unchanged.

The native gate passed all 33 door/tier combinations, covering 132 complete press-and-return cycles using 20 N at the actual sites. Every switch was also inspected at 17 stroke positions. The minimum measured stock gap was 0.500 mm, mounting separation was zero, and switch contact penetration was zero. Two negative fixtures refill the guide or detach a plate and fail the gate. Actual-force interaction tests on DB0011, DB0153 and DB0175 verify that measured button depression precedes drive commands and native opening; DB0011 also requires its latch to retract before the leaf swings.

Evidence is `out/mechanical-foundations/automatic-pads/receipt.json`, which binds source modules and each exported spec/model/XML. The source tests are `tests/test_automatic_activation_pads.py`. The optional helper arguments leave all 26 existing call/REX model outputs unchanged when both helper versions are evaluated against the same source (`existing-default-parity.json`), and those existing switches still pass their three-tier stock and native-cycle checks. This is a switch mechanism and activation-sequence proof, not a whole-door mechanical certificate or a new benchmark result.

The accompanying `clearance.json` records seven automatic swing/sliding doors passing both generic clearance gates. Its four sectional snapshots fail existing cable-anchor/track intersections, including 35 mm initial overlap; none of those failure pairs contains an activation pad. That separate sectional repair remains outside this switch signoff.

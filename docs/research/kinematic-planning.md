# Whole-body kinematic planning for DoorBench

Research snapshot: 2026-09-05. This note compares six practical methods using their official repositories, documentation, and papers. Recommendations below are engineering judgments for DoorBench, not independently reproduced performance claims. No published dataset or remote GPU node was changed during this research.

## Recommendation

Start with **Mink on CPU**, using a proper articulated adult rig, explicit foot/contact phases, and the original MuJoCo door geometry. Add short-window constrained trajectory optimization and independent acceptance checks. Consider **PyRoki** for batched trajectory refinement, and **cuRoboV2 on a new NVIDIA node** if multi-start collision planning becomes the bottleneck. Natural appearance needs a motion prior or authored motion style in addition to constraints; a feasible pose objective alone does not generate human movement.

The current local environment has Python 3.12, MuJoCo 3.12.0, NumPy 2.5.2, and SciPy 1.18.1 on macOS ARM64. The pilot subsequently installed Mink 1.3.0, qpsolvers 4.13.0 and DAQP 0.9.1. Pink, PyRoki and JAX were not installed. The published v1 `reference/humanoid.py` computes independent two-bone limb solutions around a heuristic root path. It has no articulated joint-angle model, collision constraints, foot orientation constraints, or balance optimization. Replacing its arm formula with framewise IK will not repair an impossible root or foot path.

## Practical CPU implementation

The experimental `reference/rig.py` and `reference/ik.py` now implement the first local solver: an original 38-coordinate/37-DOF adult rig with a free pelvis and 31 bounded hinges. Its neutral head top is 1.68 m and pelvis height is 0.94 m. The API preserves native door coordinates in a private combined model; supplied foot targets are ankle frames, 55 mm above the sole when upright. Position-only hand and elbow tasks use a world-space point residual, avoiding orientation-dependent translation errors from an SE(3) logarithm. Six-dimensional stance targets remain hard pose equalities.

Endpoint acceptance checks actual MuJoCo distances, joint limits, total-step velocity and foot residuals after every nonlinear solve. Internal recovery may reduce an existing collision caused by a newly supplied door pose, but an incompletely recovered endpoint remains infeasible. The implementation corrects a verified Mink 1.3 collision-bound unit mismatch and uses actual floor normals at zero-gap foot contact; dedicated regressions guard those assumptions. This local solver does not constrain acceleration or certify the interpolated trajectory. Independent saved-trajectory validation and visual review remain required before any new motion is labeled accepted; these experiments do not change the published v1 reference release.

The architecture below distinguishes the implemented local solver from broader
trajectory refinement and manipulation work. The current pipeline adds authored
footsteps, soft upright chest guidance, geometric hand targets and local retiming;
it does not yet implement oriented grasps or optimization over pose windows.
The separate validator and its saved reports define actual acceptance.

1. **Articulated original rig.** Give the existing 16 canonical landmarks an underlying free pelvis and bounded spine, shoulders, elbows, hips, knees and ankles. Add rigid foot frames and conservative torso/limb collision geometry. Represent wrist orientation as well as position. Keep dimensions explicit; equal limb lengths alone do not establish anatomical feasibility.
2. **Private combined scene.** Load the native door plus rig into a separate MuJoCo model/data pair. Copy native door coordinates by name/address. During a pose solve, prohibit the optimizer from moving the door to make the person fit. Preserve actual mesh geometry, rather than substituting an unconstrained slab or using visual-only bounds.
3. **Discrete phase and stance plan.** Choose approach side, hand, grasp frame, support foot, step locations, release and passage phases. Test a small set of stance/hand alternatives before optimizing. Door aperture and swept-volume constraints must affect the root path. A grounded contact phase uses a fixed six-dimensional foot pose; a swing phase gets a separate clearance trajectory and landing target.
4. **Constrained local IK.** Soft costs handle pelvis/chest orientation, posture and hand tracking. Hard constraints handle planted feet and prohibited DOF motion; limits enforce angle, speed and self/scene clearance. Exclude only the intended gripping hand against the specific grasped geometry. Keep the free hand, forearms and torso in collision checks. Permit foot-ground contact without permitting unrelated body-floor penetration.
5. **Trajectory refinement.** Optimize overlapping windows with pose, velocity, acceleration and jerk terms, explicit contact intervals, and consistent wrist orientations. Use multiple initializations when a local solution fails. Smoothness is a cost, not a replacement for rechecking the constraints after smoothing. A SciPy-based prototype can share MuJoCo residuals/Jacobians; PyRoki can later provide sparse/autodiff and batched optimization if profiling justifies conversion.
6. **Retiming and provenance.** If recorded door motion is too fast for bounded human motion, either reject that timing or create an explicitly retimed derivative. Native state positions can be resampled, but recorded forces and benchmark outcomes cannot be presented as a new physically executed interaction at the modified times. The moving door must be included in collision checks over time, not frozen for the complete trajectory.
7. **Independent acceptance.** Validate the saved coordinates using fresh MuJoCo data, not only the solver's residual cache. Classify accepted, infeasible, and unresolved trajectories separately. Keep the failure cause and best attempt for every requested door.

## What can be guaranteed

Use a narrowly stated acceptance contract: *within this rig, geometry, contact schedule, numerical tolerance and verification resolution*. Record the maximum residual, offending frame, geometry pair, solver convergence and source hashes. Initial proposed tolerances below are engineering settings to calibrate, not sourced physiological standards.

| Check | Proposed initial acceptance | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Joint position / velocity | Every bounded coordinate and speed within declared limits, with only explicit solver tolerance | Human comfort, actuator torque, or joint coupling not represented by the rig |
| Planted feet | <=1 mm position residual and <=0.5 degrees orientation residual; contact-point slip measured across each stance | Friction capacity or balance during acceleration |
| Hand contact | Current implementation checks position and surface proximity; orientation remains a future grasp requirement | A usable grasp, finger closure, or ability to apply the opening force |
| Collision | Positive clearance for non-contact pairs, conservative geometry, and adaptive interval subdivision near clearance minima | A formal continuous-time certificate from checking endpoints or uniformly sampled frames |
| Support | Fixed stance feet and floor support are checked; COM guidance is only a soft objective | Support-polygon containment, dynamic balance, unilateral contact forces or friction-cone satisfaction |
| Motion quality | Current checks bound sampled speed and acceleration; visual review remains separate, and jerk is not a gate | Human naturalness from a single scalar score |

Mink's collision limits are linearized inequalities. Its documentation notes that larger integration steps can invalidate the first-order approximation. Use step backtracking and re-evaluate true distances and foot poses after integration. Even dense sampling is not a mathematical continuous-collision proof unless paired with conservative motion bounds or a suitable continuous-collision algorithm. [Mink collision model](https://kevinzakka.github.io/mink/api/limits.html), [integration limits](https://kevinzakka.github.io/mink/tutorial/quickstart.html).

For a stronger future dynamics contract, the 2026 KDMR paper is instructive: it optimizes scheduled multi-point contacts with whole-body dynamics, friction and actuator-related constraints, using measured ground-reaction information. The authors' July revision still says the end-to-end pipeline will be open-sourced upon publication; no runnable licensed implementation was established in this review. DoorBench's procedural foot labels and generalized-force door recordings do not supply its measured contact inputs. Treat this as a formulation reference, not an available drop-in dependency or a proof that our current references are dynamically feasible. [KDMR paper, July 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09956v2).

## Natural style and all-door scope

Natural movement should enter as a licensed motion prior or carefully authored pose/contact library: anticipatory head/chest turn, pelvis weight transfer, contralateral arm swing, appropriately timed deceleration, wrist alignment and release. Keep collision/contact/limit constraints authoritative when the style prior conflicts. Do not blend two accepted trajectories and assume the blend remains feasible.

No cited planner guarantees a feasible operation for every possible door and fixed body. Jammed or locked tasks, undersized apertures, unreachable hardware, overhead hatches without access equipment and incompatible prescribed timings can be infeasible. Full coverage should mean every door receives a planned attempt plus explicit accepted/infeasible/unresolved status. Changing the actor stature, adding a step or choosing another operator is a task assumption that must be recorded rather than hidden.

## GPU escalation

First measure a CPU pilot across a sliding handle, lever swing, close-clearance turn, low latch and high operator. Track feasible yield, collision/contact residuals, time per frame and initialization sensitivity. A **new isolated Linux NVIDIA node** is justified for batched PyRoki or cuRoboV2 multi-start trajectory optimization once the rig, task semantics and CPU validator work. A 24–48 GB GPU is a reasonable provisional engineering choice for batched high-DOF experiments, not a verified minimum or a speed estimate. Pin code, CUDA/driver/container and robot/world conversions, and compare the GPU outputs through the same native validator. This research did not access or modify the existing RunPod node.
